# Advanced-Programming- Route Tariff and Capacity Fee Calculator

## A brief overview of the natural gas pipeline system
I work in the system usage department of a natural gas trading company. To transport natural gas through pipelines, it is necessary to **book** pipeline capacity sat cross-border interconnection points (**network points**). Each country’s gas network is managed by a Transmission System Operator (**TSO**). In addition to ensuring the security of supply, traders execute profit-oriented transactions. The core strategy is to buy gas where it is cheaper and sell it in markets where prices are higher. When calculating the profit margin for these deals, the capacity booking costs along the transport **route** are taken into account.
**Capacity booking** is conducted on the respective TSO's system for specific **product instruments**, which can be **yearly, quarterly, monthly, daily**, or within-day products. At my current company, we hold active network usage contracts in 14 different countries.

**The objective of the project is to develop an application or interface that quickly, clearly, and simply provides traders with the relevant costs. This will not only support their daily work, but also significantly reduce our department's workload.**

## 1. The demo
The app runs on the real tariffs in `data/real/` by default. The Capacity Fee Calculator covers all 21 TSOs in that file (from Amber Grid to Snam Rete Gas), each in its own currency (EUR, HUF, RON, CZK or PLN) and capacity unit (kWh/h or kWh/d); the DSO exits, storage operators and LNGCRO in the file are left out. The Route Tariff Calculator prices any route the trader picks on a map of the 22 border points between 15 countries (from NL and DE to GR and UA), for example through Serbia with FGSZ (Hungary, HUF per kWh/h), Gastrans (Serbia, EUR per kWh/h) and Bulgartransgaz (Bulgaria, EUR per kWh/d, from 2026-01-01 when its tariffs switched from BGN to EUR). The app uses Firm capacity, and Interruptible capacity only at points that have no Firm tariff (for example the north-to-south direction at Kiskundorozsma 2 and Kireevo/Zaychar). Open it at https://route-tariff-and-capacity-fee-calculator.streamlit.app (or run it locally, see [Running the MVP](#running-the-mvp)).

**Route Tariff Calculator.** I open the app in the browser and stay on the "Route Tariff Calculator" tab. Route beginning: BG; on the map, which shows only the country borders, each country's code and a marker on every border point, I click Kireevo/Zaychar and then Kiskundorozsma 2 (this is also the route the tab opens with), so the route reads "BG > Kireevo/Zaychar > RS > Kiskundorozsma 2 > HU". The same route can be built with the "Next point" list, which offers only the points on the border of the country the route has reached; "Remove last" and "Clear" undo clicks, and a point that does not border that country, or that would lead back into a country already on the route, is refused with a message such as "Not possible: Vyrava (PL - SK) does not border BG.". Booking period 2026-10-01 to 2027-03-31 (both dates inclusive). Because FGSZ prices in HUF, a "HUF per 1 EUR" box appears and I type today's rate, for example 400. A route runs hub to hub, so the app shows one table of the four network points the gas crosses: Bulgartransgaz exit Kireevo/Zaychar (BG>RS), Gastrans entry Kireevo/Zaychar (BG>RS), Gastrans exit Kiskundorozsma 2 (RS>HU) and FGSZ entry Kiskundorozsma 2 (RS>HU), each with its EIC, direction, capacity type, the products it books and its cost in EUR/MWh. Each TSO is optimised on its own, in its own currency and unit (the two Gastrans points share one product choice); here every point books "Q4 2026, Q1 2027". On the real tariffs the points cost 1.4431, 0.8494, 0.9684 and 0.4348 EUR/MWh, which add up to the route total: 3.6957 EUR/MWh. Below it the app compares each TSO with the next shorter products it could have booked, here "Without quarterly products": 3.9913 EUR/MWh, so the quarters save 0.2956 EUR/MWh. For a whole gas year (2026-10-01 to 2027-09-30) every point books "GY 2026/27", the route costs 2.7400 EUR/MWh, and the comparison is "Without yearly products" (3.3411, a saving of 0.6010). Without the rate the FGSZ cell stays empty and the route total waits for it; a route whose TSOs all price in EUR, such as BG > Kireevo/Zaychar > RS, needs no rate at all. To compare, I click Clear and then Ruse/Giurgiu and Csanádpalota: the route BG > Ruse/Giurgiu > RO > Csanádpalota > HU asks for a RON and a HUF rate, and at 5 RON and 400 HUF per EUR (example rates) its points cost 0.7340, 1.1612, 1.0772 and 0.4348 EUR/MWh, 3.4072 in total. Austria shows how a country with two TSOs works: HU > Mosonmagyaróvár > AT > Tarvisio/Arnoldstein > IT enters at Gas Connect Austria and leaves at Trans Austria Gasleitung, and each is optimised on its own. If the period misses a few days at the edge of a month or quarter, the optimiser may **over-book**: it buys the whole month or quarter when that is cheaper than covering only the booked days. For example, for 2026-10-02 to 2026-12-30 every point reads "Q4 2026 (+2 days)" and a note explains the over-booking; the route costs 3.6415 EUR/MWh, because per-MWh figures still divide by the booked days only. A closed "Details" section lists every point's products with their dates, the tariff row each price came from and the exact tariff in the point's own unit (e.g. 0.1211087 EUR per kWh/d for Bulgartransgaz in Q4 2026). Starting from HU and clicking Kiskundorozsma 2 and then Kireevo/Zaychar lists the three Interruptible points with a warning that the TSO can interrupt that capacity.

**Capacity Fee Calculator.** I switch to the "Capacity Fee Calculator" tab. First I choose what I book: a **Bundled** point (the exit of one TSO and the entry of the next at the same border, booked together; 43 of them, wherever both sides are Firm), a **Single side** (any one exit or entry, chosen by TSO and point) or an **LNG entry** (14 terminals). I keep "Bundled" and the point "Mosonmagyaróvár AT>HU (Gas Connect Austria + FGSZ)", product instrument "Quarter", 2026 Q4, requested capacity 50,000 kWh/d (I switch the "Unit" list next to the capacity box from its default, kWh/h, to kWh/d). Both sides price 2083.33 kWh/h, each in its own currency: the Gas Connect Austria exit costs 2,605.83 EUR (invoiced 868.61 EUR for each of October, November and December; Gas Connect Austria bills its tariff rounded to 4 decimals, so 1.25082191 EUR per kWh/h in the file is priced as 1.2508), the FGSZ entry 782,558 HUF (260,853, 260,853 and 260,852 HUF). FGSZ, Eustream, Gas Connect Austria and NET4GAS invoice each month of a gas year or quarter as 1/12 or 1/3 of it, and Gastrans each month of a quarter as 1/3 of it, leftover cents or forints going to the first months; the other TSOs split the invoice by calendar days. Each invoice adds up exactly to its total. Because one side is in HUF, a "HUF per 1 EUR" box appears; typing 395.5 gives the bundle total, 4,584.48 EUR. A bundle with both sides in EUR, such as Kulata/Sidirokastron BG>GR, shows its EUR total straight away; one like Csanádpalota RO>HU asks for both a RON and a HUF rate. Switching to "Single side", TSO FGSZ, point "Kiskundorozsma 2 RS>HU (21Z000000000505P)" gives the same FGSZ figure on its own, 782,558 HUF, with no FX box. Points priced per kWh/d (Bulgartransgaz, ICGB, TAP, Gas TSO of Ukraine, Plinacro) use the 50,000 kWh/d as it is. In kWh/h, the default unit, 2,083.33 kWh/h is the same booking as 50,000 kWh/d (24 hours a day), and at a kWh/d point a typed kWh/h amount is multiplied by 24. For "LNG entry", Świnoujście (GAZ-SYSTEM) costs 11,703.78 PLN for the same quarter. At Baumgarten only Gas Connect Austria's WAG point is offered on the Austrian side. Most TSOs outside Hungary change their tariffs on 1 January, so a product that crosses a tariff change is priced part by part, weighted by days, or by months for the TSOs above: for the gas year 2026/27 the Gas Connect Austria exit Mosonmagyaróvár AT>HU shows both tariffs, "3.9700 / 4.1000" EUR per kWh/h, costs 8,473.96 EUR for 50,000 kWh/d (2083.33 x (3 x 3.97 + 9 x 4.10) / 12), and its invoice bills 1/12 of the 2026 tariff for October to December (689.24 EUR each) and 1/12 of the 2027 tariff from January (711.81 or 711.80 EUR). The route calculator weights a product across a tariff change by days for every TSO and uses the tariffs unrounded, since it turns them into EUR/MWh.

## 2. The shape
* in: a CSV/Excel export of network capacity tariffs, plus a user query specifying the route's beginning and ending country (or, on tab 2, a TSO and network point), booking period, product instrument 
* out: an optimized unit tariff breakdown, the total calculated cost for the requested volume
* on screen: Two tabs, on the first one a calculator panel with a "Route beginning" dropdown, a minimal map on which the trader clicks the route's border points (or a "Next point" list), dates and a results dashboard listing every network point on the route and the optimal (cheapest) product combination per TSO, with the route total in EUR/MWh. On the other tab a booking type (bundled point, single side or LNG entry), the point, an input field for the capacity volume, product instrument and a results dashboard showing the cost of the capacity in each side's own currency, the calculated invoice for each month, and for a bundle an optional EUR total.

## 3. The size
### What the first useful version does:
* Parse and load the standard tariff Excel/CSV file into a queryable structure.
* Find the cheapest combination of yearly/quarterly/monthly/daily products that covers a specific time period, over-booking whole months, quarters or gas years outside the period when that is cheaper.
* Calculate the total payable fee based on a user-defined capacity volume and break it down into monthly costs.
* Prices capacity at the points of all 21 TSOs in the real tariff file, bundled or one side at a time, and prices hub-to-hub routes the trader picks on a map of the border points between 15 countries

### What it explicitly does not do this term:
* Live API integration with ENTSOG, Regional Booking Platform or TSO websites for automatic data fetching (files will be uploaded manually).
* Multi-currency conversion via real-time external exchange APIs (it calculates in the source currency; the route's EUR/MWh and a bundle's EUR total use FX rates the trader types in).
* Searching for the cheapest path between two countries (the trader picks the border points; each TSO on the route is optimised separately), and routes through the TAP and ICGB pipelines.
* Calculating the necessary financial security that needs to be held at the TSO by the System User for a given capacity booking request.
* Doesn't calculate with within-day capacity products.

## 4. How we would know it works
* Given a specific capacity volume for a quarterly period, the sum of the generated monthly cost breakdown equals the exact calculated total cost.
* Given a not acceptable route, instead of crashing, it displays a message saying the route is not possible.
* Given a booking request that crosses the October 1st gas year boundary, it correctly splits the period, applying the old tariff up to September 30 and the new tariff from October 1 onwards.

## 5. What could stop this
* The logic for mixing different countries and TSOs capacity fee calculators might have too many irregular methods to put all together in the same way.
* The structure of the source Excel file might be inconsistent (e.g., unexpected empty cells,) which could break the parser.
* Handling leap years and exact timezone boundaries in the booking period to parsing might prove unexpectedly complex.

The project now uses the real tariff data, committed as `data/real/tariffs_real.xlsx`. The tariffs are public on the TSOs' websites, and my employer allowed me to use them. The file is the work export without its author and comment columns, and the app opens it by default. The synthetic sample in `data/sample/` is kept, because the earlier tests were built on it. The country borders on the route map (`data/map/countries.geojson`) are cut from Natural Earth's 1:50m Admin 0 countries (v5.1.2), which is public domain; the border points' positions are typed in by hand in `core/map_points.py`.

## Running the MVP
The app is hosted on Streamlit Community Cloud: **https://route-tariff-and-capacity-fee-calculator.streamlit.app**. It opens the real tariffs by default; another tariff file can be uploaded in the sidebar. If nobody has opened it for a while it shows a "gone to sleep" page; click the button to wake it (about a minute).

To run it locally instead:
```
pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m streamlit run app.py     # uses the real tariffs in data/real/
python -m pytest                   # unit, app and acceptance tests
```
To use another file, such as the synthetic sample, point the app at it with the `FGSZ_TARIFF_FILE` environment variable (PowerShell: `$env:FGSZ_TARIFF_FILE = "data/sample/tariffs_sample.xlsx"`, then `python -m streamlit run app.py`), or upload it in the sidebar for one session. The raw work export, which still has colleagues' names in it, stays in `data/private/` (git-ignored, never committed). The parser reports missing columns and unusable rows in plain language instead of crashing.

## The Capacity Fee Workbook (tab 2 in Excel)
`excel/capacity_fee_calculator.xlsx` does everything the Capacity Fee Calculator tab does, in one Excel file that anyone can open from a shared folder: dropdowns for the booking type, TSO, point, product and period, the capacity and its unit, then each side's price, total and monthly invoice, and a bundle's EUR total once its exchange rates are typed in. It is a plain `.xlsx` with formulas only (no macros, so no "Enable content" prompt), for Microsoft 365 or Excel 2021, and works in any Excel language; only the yellow cells are meant to be edited (the sheet is protected without a password).

The workbook is built from the tariff file, never edited by hand. After a new tariff export:
```
python build_workbook.py                          # from data/real/tariffs_real.xlsx
python build_workbook.py path/to/export.xlsx      # or from another file
python -m pytest -m excel                         # recalculate it in Excel and compare it with the app
```
The build uses the app's own tariff loading, point catalog and rules, and stops with the app's message (writing nothing) on a file the app rejects. The Excel check needs Windows with Excel installed; it fills in the workbook for every point, product and capacity unit and compares each figure with the app (it is skipped in a plain `python -m pytest`). Then copy the new file over the one on the server; marking the server copy read-only keeps colleagues from saving over it.

A change to a tab 2 fee rule must also be made in the workbook (`core/workbook.py`) and in the `excel-fee-workbook` spec; the Excel check fails until both agree.
