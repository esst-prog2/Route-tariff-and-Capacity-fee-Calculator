## Problem Statement
Homework 9 asks for a promo page on GitHub Pages: what the program is for, who it is for, and how to try it, with a link to the program because it runs online (https://route-tariff-and-capacity-fee-calculator.streamlit.app). This term the readers are a classmate, the teacher and a stranger, and none of them knows gas trading. The app itself is for gas traders. So the page has to explain the problem in one plain line before it pitches anything.

## What Makes This Cool
The map click. A trader clicks two border points (BG > Kireevo/Zaychar > RS > Kiskundorozsma 2 > HU) and in seconds has the cheapest product mix for each of the three pipeline operators and the whole route's cost: 3.6957 EUR/MWh. Today that same question goes to the system usage team as an email, and someone works it out by hand from each operator's tariff sheet.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: One screen
Headline, one line on the problem, a button, three bullets. Ruled out: gives a stranger no reason to trust the numbers.
### Approach B: Short story page (chosen)
Headline → the problem → before/after → the three tabs, each with one real example and its number → how to try it → why the numbers can be trusted → what it does not do → footer.
### Approach C: Follow one molecule

## Recommended Approach
Approach B, chosen by the user. The page copy, section by section (wording may be tightened in the design steps; the facts and numbers may not change):

**1. Name and headline**
- Name: Route Tariff and Capacity Fee Calculator
- Headline: "Click the border points on a map, see what the whole route costs, and book capacity without asking anyone."
- Under it: "For gas traders who move gas across the borders of Central and Eastern Europe."
- Button: "Open the calculator" → https://route-tariff-and-capacity-fee-calculator.streamlit.app

**2. The problem, in one line for strangers**
"Gas that crosses a border pays each country's pipeline operator for the capacity it books, and every operator has its own price list, currency, units and products: year, quarter, month or day."

**3. Before and after**
- Before: the trader emails the system usage team, and someone works the cost out by hand from each operator's tariff sheet.
- After: the trader clicks the route and has the answer in seconds, with the cheapest mix of products already chosen.

**4. Three questions, one tool** (one card per tab, each with one real example)
- Route Tariff Calculator: "What does this route cost?" BG > Kireevo/Zaychar > RS > Kiskundorozsma 2 > HU, booked 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, at 400 HUF per EUR: 3.6957 EUR/MWh. Booking quarters instead of months saves 0.2956 EUR/MWh.
- Capacity Fee Calculator: "What will the invoices be?" 50,000 kWh/d at Mosonmagyaróvár AT>HU for Q4 2026: 2,605.83 EUR to Gas Connect Austria and 782,558 HUF to FGSZ, each split into the three monthly invoices they will send.
- Bank Guarantee Calculator: "How much guarantee do I need?" 100,000 kWh/h at Eustream's Balassagyarmat SK>HU for Q4 2026, with the gas price index CEGHIX at 30 EUR/MWh, as booked capacity (not at the auction stage): a capacity fee of 361,344.00 EUR, and a guarantee of 280,104.80 EUR.

**5. Try it**
- Button again, and: "Free, no login. If nobody has used it for a while it is asleep: click the wake-up button and give it about a minute."
- "Check it yourself: on the first tab start from BG, click Kireevo/Zaychar and then Kiskundorozsma 2, book 2026-10-01 to 2027-03-31 and type 400 HUF per EUR. You should see 3.6957 EUR/MWh."
- Smaller: "Or run it on your own computer: the code and instructions are on GitHub." → https://github.com/esst-prog2/Route-tariff-and-Capacity-fee-Calculator

**6. Why you can trust the numbers**
- The real tariffs of 21 transmission system operators, public on each operator's own website and collected by hand into one file; routes run across 22 border points between 15 countries.
- Over 400 automated tests check the app, including every example on this page.
- The examples use the tariff file of 29 October 2026; when the file is updated, the page's figures are checked again (the tests on the real file fail first if they changed).
- Each operator's own invoicing rule: some bill a quarter as three equal thirds, others by calendar days, and every invoice adds up exactly to its total.

**7. What it does not do (yet)**
- It does not book capacity for you: it tells you what to book and what it will cost; you book on the operator's platform yourself.
- It does not fetch tariffs live: the tariff file is updated by hand.
- It does not choose the path for you: you pick the border points, and it optimises each operator on the route.
- The guarantee tab knows only Eustream's rule so far.
- Exchange rates are typed in, not fetched; no within-day products.

**8. Footer**
"Built for the Advanced Programming course at ELTE, 2026." · GitHub link. No personal name.
