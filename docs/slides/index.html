This is the check where I was in control. Before any code ran, I opened the tariff file in Excel and read the two yearly prices myself: 5100.14 together. That number went into a test. The agent's optimiser said its plan was the cheapest, but it cost 6478.73, 27 percent more, because it never considered yearly products. The test went red, the optimiser was fixed, and now it is green.

What I learned

"Cheapest" is a rule of the trade, not of the maths. I had to ask a person.

  1. 1 OctMy teacher's review asked: is it really the cheapest? I opened a spike on 1,050 bookings.
  2. 1 Oct428 of them overpaid. The agent's code only bought the exact days asked for, never the whole month.
  3. 3 OctFixing the code was easy. Whether a trader may pay for days they won't use, only the trade knows. I asked a capacity manager at work.

"It is allowed to overbook capacity … it simply will not be used on the days that do not contain the gas deal."PLANNING_LOG.md · 3 Oct 2026

2–31 December, HUF per kWh/h:
1767.2 day by day
749.6 all of December
136% too much, the worst case
The moment is in my planning log, on 3 October. My teacher asked whether the "cheapest combination" really is the cheapest. A spike over 1,050 bookings showed 428 of them overpaid: booking 2 to 31 December day by day cost more than twice as much as booking the whole of December. The agent could compute both prices, but whether traders may book days they will not use is a rule of the trade, so I asked a capacity manager at work. The answer was yes, and the optimiser now over-books when that is cheaper.
